\subsection{Keyspace}

Parameters that do not change the map do not enlarge the keyspace. Write this as a short paragraph and refer to the exhaustive check in the artifact.}

\subsection{Diffusion}
\todo{Flipping one bit of one input byte changes exactly \DiffusionMean{} output byte, in every one of \DiffusionTrials{} trials. A block cipher over the same 256 bytes would change about half. The layer therefore adds no diffusion, and any statistical property observed at the output is the cipher's, not the layer's. One paragraph.}

\subsection{Security}
\todo{The argument, stated once and cleanly. If $T_k$ is public, or $k$ is transmitted with the ciphertext (as it must be if the receiver does not share it), then any adversary against $\mathsf{Enc}'$ applies $T_k$ to its chosen plaintexts and $T_k^{-1}$ to any recovered plaintext and becomes an adversary against $\mathsf{Enc}$ with identical advantage; conversely $\mathsf{Enc}'$ inherits every property of $\mathsf{Enc}$. The composite is exactly as secure as the cipher behind it. If $k$ is kept secret and never transmitted, it adds at most $\log_2$ of the keyspace, here \KeyspaceLog{} bits. Cite the standard hybrid argument \cite{bellare2000ae}. No new theorem is needed.}
